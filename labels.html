What Texas requires on your label

Every template has a spot for each of these. Rules change, so check the Texas DSHS cottage food page before you sell.

  • Your business name, and your address or DSHS registration number
  • The common name of the product, like "pecan pralines"
  • Any major allergens, like milk, eggs, wheat, soy, peanuts, tree nuts or sesame
  • The statement: “This product was produced in a private residence that is not subject to governmental licensing or inspection.”
  • Foods that need refrigeration: the date made, and the safe-handling statement in at least 12-point type (on the label or the receipt)
  • Pickled, fermented and canned goods: a batch number

I design and print labels from the information you provide. You're responsible for the accuracy of that information and for following Texas cottage food law. This isn't legal advice.

Label questions

Don't see yours? Call me or send a message.

Do I need to register with the state?

Only in some cases: if you sell food that has to stay refrigerated, if you sell through a cottage food vendor, or if you want a registration number on your label instead of your home address. Check the Texas DSHS cottage food page for the current rules.

Can you tell me if my product is allowed?

No. I design and print labels; I’m not a lawyer or a health inspector. The Texas DSHS cottage food page lists what you can sell and is the place to check.

Who is responsible for what's on the label?

You are. You give me the product name, ingredients, allergens and your address or registration number, and you approve a proof before anything prints. I lay it out so every required item is there and readable.
